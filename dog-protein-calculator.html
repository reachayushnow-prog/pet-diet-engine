What does this dog protein calculator actually calculate?

This tool estimates a daily energy requirement using the standard RER equation 70 × body weight(kg)0.75 and an educational life-stage/activity factor. It then applies a protein-per-energy benchmark from AAFCO's dog nutrient profiles.

For adult-maintenance dog food, AAFCO's profile uses a minimum of 45 g crude protein per 1,000 kcal of metabolizable energy. For growth and reproduction, the minimum is 56.3 g per 1,000 kcal. These are nutrient-profile benchmarks for complete and balanced dog foods, not personalized prescriptions for an individual dog.

How to use the result

1. Start with the benchmark

Use the result to understand the approximate protein amount represented by the selected energy intake.

2. Check the food label

Pet-food labels normally list a minimum crude-protein percentage in the guaranteed analysis. Moisture matters when comparing protein percentages between foods.

3. Don't judge food by protein alone

A complete diet must provide many nutrients in appropriate amounts and ratios. A higher protein percentage does not automatically make a food better for every dog.

4. Consider life stage

Puppies and growing dogs have different nutrient requirements from adult-maintenance dogs. Pregnancy and nursing also require special consideration.

Important limitations

Educational estimate only. This calculator does not diagnose disease, determine an individualized therapeutic diet, or prove that a homemade recipe is complete and balanced. Calorie needs vary between individual dogs. Puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs, senior dogs, underweight/overweight dogs, and dogs with medical conditions may need individualized guidance from a veterinarian or qualified veterinary nutrition professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein does an adult dog need?

Protein needs vary with life stage, energy intake, diet and health. As a food-profile benchmark, AAFCO adult-maintenance dog food profiles use a minimum of 45 g crude protein per 1,000 kcal ME, or 18% on a dry-matter basis.

Do puppies need more protein?

Growing puppies have higher nutrient requirements than adult-maintenance dogs. AAFCO's growth and reproduction profile uses a minimum of 56.3 g crude protein per 1,000 kcal ME and 22.5% on a dry-matter basis.

Does a high-protein dog food mean it is healthier?

Not necessarily. Protein quantity is only one part of nutritional adequacy. The food also needs appropriate amounts and ratios of other essential nutrients for the dog's life stage and condition.

Can I use this calculator to formulate homemade dog food?

No. Protein is only one nutrient. A homemade diet requires appropriate amounts of many nutrients, and an unbalanced recipe can create nutritional problems. Use this tool for education and discuss homemade diets with a veterinary nutrition professional.

What does “crude protein” mean on a dog-food label?

Crude protein is a standardized analytical measure used in pet-food labeling. A guaranteed analysis normally lists a minimum crude-protein percentage along with other nutrient guarantees.
